THE SESSION'S PREDICTION (the session's own reasoning):

1. DARK LINES (a line whose light is exactly 0 after the water level), counted at the new positions of the 30 prompts.
   The water level puts the brightest line of a light at 15 places, so a line is dark only where it sits under about
   1/65,536 of the brightest line.
   - qkv and fc (the light is the stream): under 0.5% of the 768 lines at every layer. The stream's brightest channels
     are tens to a few hundred times the usual line, so the dark cut-off sits far below where lines gather.
   - proj (the light is the arrival's output): under 0.2% at every layer.
   - mproj (the light is the bend's output): the bend gives the far-negative values almost nothing, so lines whose
     value went below about -3.3 to -3.9 (at the gauge's scale) come out dark: about 2-8% of the 3,072 lines at most
     layers, most likely about 4%, a few layers higher.
   - all 48 plates together: 0.7-3% of the plate bytes, most likely about 1.5% (mproj is a third of the plate bytes).
   - Where the dark lines sit does not matter to the bytes: each line's row is skipped on its own, so the saving is
     the count alone. A shuffle of the lines would keep every count; it is not a control here and is not run.

2. THE RULE FOR USING DARK LINES, fixed now, before the plate kernel is timed and before any words/s round:
   the test (skip a line's row when its light is 0) is switched on for a plate kind (qkv, proj, fc, mproj; one
   decision a kind, all 12 layers together) only if
       (mean dark lines a new position at that kind, summed over its 12 plates) x (row bytes) / (the measured plate
       read rate of the new kernel)
     > (the measured cost of the test at that kind) = GPU time of the new kernel with the test present but never
       firing (it compares with a value the light never takes) minus GPU time without the test,
   both from one bench at one real position, many passes each. If the cost is below what the timer can see, the
   rule counts it as zero and the kind needs at least one dark line a new position on average.

3. THE PLATE READ (same arithmetic, every pile identical):
   - The new kernel for one new position: each lane reads 16 bytes (8 neighbouring piles of a line) so a simdgroup
     reads 512 contiguous bytes of one line; each lane carries 8 lines (all 8 rows asked for before any is summed);
     8 simdgroups of a threadgroup carry 64 lines and add their piles in threadgroup memory; each threadgroup adds
     its piles into the pile's two narrow 32-bit cells (bottom 15 digits, the rest) with whole-number atomic adds,
     which are exact in any order. The separate step that added the slices (addparts1) is gone; the cells are
     cleared by the water-level step just before, and the pile is formed once, where it is used.
   - Plate read: 65 -> 85-95 GB/s (the top read's 93 is reached with 1-byte loads and 600K threads; here fewer threads
     but 8 x 16 bytes in flight each).
   - 12 layers: 3,150 -> 2,350-2,600 us (bytes 170 MB at about 90 GB/s = about 1,890 us, plus about 216 other steps
     at about 2-3 us; 48 steps fewer than now).
   - words/s after the prompt: +15 to +25% over base2_stream_fr.swift planes=3 (about 285-310 from about 249).
   - words/s with the prompt: a smaller rise, +10 to +20% (the prompt runs on the machine's own kernels).
   - the top read unchanged and exact as before (its kernels are not touched).
