THE TIPS AND THE BLANK SLATE. Written 2026-10-06, before any measuring run of tips_blank/.

HIS WORDS (2026-10-06), the only words quoted as his:
  "I really feel like the tips are the only thing the model learned and needs and it's because all the non tips are
   the blank slate somehowz"

HIS PICTURE, RESTATED (the session's words, not his)
  Every pile is the light's places meeting the plate's places, line by line. At one place pair (light place a, plate
  place b) every line is an up meeting (the two places agree) or a down meeting (they disagree). Ups and downs pair off;
  what is left is the TIP, T = ups - downs = N - 2d. The pile is the tips alone (the tip identity: 15,439,710 of
  15,439,710 GPT-2 piles). The meetings that paired off are the NON-TIPS. His sentence: the tips are all the building
  made and all the machine needs, and the non-tips are the blank slate, somehow.

THE SESSION'S READING (the task's, kept as given): two parts.
  (i) Amount: the tips alone give the answer. The tip identity makes "the tips are all it needs" true by construction
      (rule 13.4), so it is not tested again; it is checked at every position here only as the exactness check.
  (ii) Identity: are the non-tips the blank slate? Literal: the paired-off work is the step-0 blank's work. "Somehow":
      the paired-off work behaves exactly like a blank slate's, whatever made it.

ANALYSIS BEFORE THE RUN (the session's)
  1. Per meeting, paired off + |tip| = N, the line count. So "the non-tips are blank-sized" and "the tips are
     blank-sized" are one statement read from two sides (13.4). On one grid, with 2^d weights, the paired-off mass
     cannot stay at the blank's while the tips grow: wherever the tips grow, the paired-off mass falls by exactly as much.
  2. The coin-toss law. At any place pair where one side's places are fair coins along the lines, independent of the
     other side, the tip is a coin toss's leftover: E|T| = N C(N, N/2) / 2^N, a share 3.53% of the lines at 512,
     2.88% at 768, 2.49% at 1,024, 1.76% at 2,048, 1.44% at 3,072, 7.04% at 128. The blank, a fresh bell draw and the
     finished plate all pair off at that rate there, whatever made them. That part of the measurement must return the
     coin toss (13.3); it cannot tell whether the non-tips ARE the blank.
  3. The places that carry a pattern along the lines are the signs. A value's places above its own top 1 all copy its
     sign (up/hole form: s_a = sign x (top places all holes)). On the plate that is places 13-15 for most weights; on
     the light it runs much lower, because each position's largest value sets its power and most values are far below
     it. Only place pairs where both sides copy signs can leave the coin toss: their tip is the lines where the light's
     sign and the plate's sign agree minus where they disagree. Those place pairs fill the top diagonals.
  4. Two things can make the finished top tips depart from the coin toss:
     (a) a standing pattern: lines whose light keeps one sign at every position, meeting plate rows that keep one sign
         over all neurons. Seen during the timing run (see "looked at before writing" below): at the Pythia-70m word
         table, 494 of 512 light lanes keep one sign at all 622 positions, 402 of 512 table rows keep one sign over
         99% of the 50,304 words, and the two agree (correlation of the patterns 0.997).
     (b) the token's own pattern: a neuron strongly lit at one position.
     The light shuffled across its lines (same values) breaks both; a blank or a fresh draw has neither.
  5. The blank on the finished light: the blank's rows have random signs, so (a) is absent, and (b) is there only as far
     as the finished plate kept the blank's pattern and the light leans into it. In Pythia the plates keep s = 9-28%
     (70m) and 7-67% (160m) of the blank's pattern, correlation 0.02-0.25 and 0.02-0.36. The excess of |tip| over the
     coin toss grows as the square of the lean, so the blank's excess should be at most about 6% (70m) and 13% (160m) of
     the finished's, at the same diagonal.
  6. The blank on its own light: each plate is its own draw and never shaped the light it meets (that light comes from
     the other plates), so it is a coin toss at every diagonal, the top ones included.
  7. Literal form: Pythia-70m's finished plates hold 0.06% of the blank (160m: 1.2%). On the shared grid that is a few
     grains of a weight: the finished plate's digits above the lowest few places are not the blank's digits, so the
     meetings that pair off in the finished machine are not the blank's meetings. This is known before the run (from
     the shave); the run measures the "somehow" form.
