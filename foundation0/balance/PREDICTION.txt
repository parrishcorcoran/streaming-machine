THE COUNT (the session's set-up, not his words):
  Each plate's values are written as whole numbers on one power of two per plate, the same power for the starting
  weights (step0) and the finished weights (step143000) of that plate, chosen so that neither passes 16 places
  (|value| <= 32767, the 16 places this machine already uses: 15 places of size and the sign).
  - sign and size: an UP is a 1 in the size of a positive weight, a DOWN is a 1 in the size of a negative weight.
    Places 0-14 carry size.
  - signed digits (second form): each whole number written with +1 and -1 digits, no two non-zero digits side by side
    (one such writing per number). An UP is a +1 digit, a DOWN is a -1 digit. Places 0-15.
  Coin-toss width: for one place, each weight with a 1 there gives +1 or -1, so a fair draw leaves ups - downs about
  sqrt(ups + downs). z = (ups - downs)/sqrt(ups + downs); a fair coin gives |z| about 1, beyond 3 in 0.27% of cases.
  For a whole plate, or one neuron, or one line: net = sum of the values against sqrt(sum of squares)
  (spread x sqrt(count)).
  Reference: a fresh bell-curve draw, same spread and same shape as each plate, on the same power of two, counted
  the same way. Shuffle control (per neuron and per line only): the plate's own values shuffled over the plate.
  The whole-plate counts do not change under any shuffle (they hold every value), so for those the fresh draw is
  the only reference.

PREDICTION (the session's):
 1. Step 0: every place of every plate balances within the coin-toss width (|z| mostly under 2; a few over 2 among
    the hundreds of place counts, as a fair coin gives about 5%; essentially none over 3), the whole-plate net within
    about 3 widths, and about 0.27% of neurons and of lines beyond 3 widths, the same as the fresh draw. Equal ups and
    downs, up to a leftover about the square root of the count. Both forms (sign and size, signed digits) the same.
 2. Step 143000: most places still balance, but some plates and some neurons carry an imbalance far beyond the
    coin toss, most likely in the word tables and the MLP plates. That excess is part of what was built.
 3. (the session's further detail) Where a finished plate is off balance, it is off at the high places, the ones
    that carry the size; the lowest places stay at the coin toss even on the finished plates.
 4. (further) The word tables carry the largest excess along the lane: each hidden lane's weights over all words
    have a net far beyond the coin toss (the finished output word table spread across lanes was 101x the shuffle
    in check_step0 for 70m). Per word, a smaller share is off.
 5. (further) Shuffle control: on the finished plates, shuffling the plate's values over the plate brings the
    per-neuron and per-line shares back to about the fresh draw's, unless the whole plate itself is off balance.

6. PREDICTION for balance_even_part.py (written before it runs).
   What it reads: every set of weights that spans the stream's lanes, its sum over those lanes (its even part: what
   an even light on every lane meets). Writes into the stream: input word table per word, attention-out per line,
   MLP-out per line. Reads from the stream: query/key/value per neuron, MLP-in per neuron, output word table per word.
   On the shared grid, start and finished are whole numbers on the same scale, so the finished even part can be
   set against the start's even part of the same row.
   The session's reasoning: the water level takes out each light's level before every read, so a write's even part
   is never met by any later read. If nothing builds it, the finished even part of each write is the start's own
   leftover, the same row's, carried through (possibly shrunk); the rest of the row grew, which is why it looks
   more even than the coin toss.
   PREDICTION:

     ratio finished/start between 0 and 1.1; the root-mean-square of the even part in start coin-toss widths no
