HIS PICTURE, RESTATED (the session's words, not his)
  Nearly everything that meets at a plate is flat: ups and holes that pair off and cancel. The flat is on both sides,
  in the light (which comes from the embedding table) and in the plate. Skip it on both sides, and what is left is the
  extra photons above the flat, added at every gate they are in.
  In the confirmed group machine (base2_groups.py, 19.40% of plain):
   - the light's flat cancels inside every ready packet: a random group's ups and holes are paired once a token, and
     every gate whose sheet names that pattern gets the pairing for free;
   - the plate's flat is skipped by the flip: a pattern's complement is the opposite packet, so half the table is free;
   - each gate, at each up/hole place, adds the one packet its sheet names.
  Pairing the light's flat once costs a table of 2^(g-1) packets a group, each written once a token. That cost is
  shared only by the gates reading that light. Pick 1 keeps the table no bigger than its readers can use: g = 11 at
  proj and mproj (768 gates read one light), g = 12 at qkv (2,304) and fc (3,072). Nothing else changes.

WHAT THIS CANNOT DO (the session's, in his picture's terms, written before the run)
  After the table is sized, the flat that is left sits inside each gate: the packets from different groups pair off
  with each other at every place (design 1 measured 98.0-98.3% of the packet photons at 768 lines, 99.5% at mproj,
  and the shuffle gives the same). That pairing belongs to the gate alone: the sheets are coin tosses, so no two gates
  hold the same selection of packets and there is nothing to share or store. A counting machine has to form each
  pairing that a real interference medium would form on one detector for free. So this build moves 19.40% to about
  18.86%; it does not move toward the "about 3%", which is a share of up/hole meetings (256 a weight, about 8x plain)
  and is the coin-toss value fixed by the number of lines.
